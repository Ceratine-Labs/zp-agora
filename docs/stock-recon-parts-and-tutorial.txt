AGORA STOCK RECON: PARTS DEFINITION AND TUTORIAL
Text edition | 30 October 2026

Stock reconciliation compares what a counting area started with, what it
received, what the tills sold and what staff counted at the end. Agora then
proposes count corrections, identifies issue problems and keeps unresolved
differences visible for investigation.

This guide describes the current local Agora implementation. It is a reading
and training guide; preparing it did not run a reconciliation or change stock.
Screen availability and write actions depend on the user's access.

PART 1: THE STOCK FIGURES

Site: The branch whose stock is being checked. Stock from different sites is
not balanced together.

Counting area: A stock-counting section within a site, such as a shop or bakery.
An item can appear in more than one area. Its area matters when reading a count,
an issue or a variance.

Stock item and unit: The product being reconciled and the unit its quantities
use. A quantity of 12 means 12 of that item's unit; it does not automatically
mean 12 packs. Compare like units.

Shift: One item's stock record for a date and shift in a counting area. A row
holds its opening, issues, closing and POS quantity.

Opening: The quantity at the start of the shift. Normally it should equal the
previous shift's closing for the same item and area.

Issued: Stock recorded as supplied into that counting area during the shift.
Here, an "issue" is a stock movement into the area, not a problem or a sale.
An omitted or duplicated issue changes the stock available on paper.

Closing: The physical quantity recorded at the end of the shift. It normally
becomes the next shift's opening.

POS quantity: The quantity sold according to the point-of-sale records linked
to the stock item. This is a quantity, not sales revenue.

Stock used according to the counts = Opening + Issued - Closing.
Variance = POS quantity - (Opening + Issued - Closing).

Short: A negative variance. More stock disappeared according to the counts
than the POS records explain. It needs investigation; the number alone does
not establish theft, responsibility or a staff charge.

Over: A positive variance. The counts imply less stock used than the POS sold.
Possible explanations include a counting error, a missing issue or incorrect
source records. "Over" is the sign of the reconciliation difference; it does
not by itself prove that extra stock was physically found.

Zero variance: The quantities agree on this basis. It does not prove that all
the underlying records are correct, because errors can offset each other.

Variance value: The quantity difference expressed in money using the run's
saved selling price. It is not a cost valuation or an approved staff charge.
Keep quantity and value separate, especially across different items.

PART 2: HOW THE RECORDS FIT TOGETHER

Period or window: The dates included in a balancing run. Choose From; Agora
calculates To as six days later, capped at today. The initial choice is the
last completed Monday-to-Sunday week. A different From date can start on any day.

Chain: The consecutive shifts for one item in one counting area at one site.
Moving a closing count also affects the next opening, so the shifts must be
read together. The proposals screen summarises each item/area chain; opening
it reveals the individual shifts and any breaks or segments.

Anchor: A count held fixed at the edge of the balancing calculation. Within a
continuous balancing segment, the first opening and latest active closing
anchor the result. Opening repairs and breaks are assessed before balancing.

Chain break: An opening differs from the previous closing. Agora reports and
proposes repairing the opening. With the current default, a break of up to
3 units can remain within the chain; larger breaks separate the balancing
segments. The 3-unit setting is not permission to ignore a shortage.

Active shift: A shift with stock activity or count movement relevant to the
calculation. Dormant shifts have no such movement. A run can contain many
records but too few active shifts to redistribute a difference meaningfully.

Incomplete day: A day with missing shifts, missing closing counts or unposted
POS under the completeness checks. The default holds it and later days out of
the calculation. Check the actual included period before interpreting totals.

Original or "as counted": The original opening, issued, closing and POS
quantities recorded in PumpIT. Agora calculates from these, with its explicit
correction rule for a grossly mis-keyed issue. Original is the baseline; it is
not a guarantee that staff captured it correctly.

Current or amended: The figures PumpIT holds now, including earlier changes.
They can differ from the originals before Agora creates this run.

Proposed or "after proposed balancing": The result Agora calculates for this
saved run. It can include count repairs, duplicate removals and missing-issue
corrections. It remains a proposal until the relevant rows are committed.

Count drift: A difference between an original count and the current count.
It identifies a change to investigate; it does not automatically mean a loss.

Balancing: Moving a count difference between neighbouring shifts while
preserving the segment's anchored stock position. Count balancing alone
cannot remove the segment's net shortage. Changing an issue changes the total
and is recorded separately as an issue correction.

Residual short: The shortage left after the applicable corrections and
balancing. Do not expect every chain to finish at zero.

Issue multiple: The expected quantity per issue or pack for an item and area.
It helps identify whole-pack differences and unusual issue quantities. It is
reference data, not a number to guess from a product description.

PART 3: THE SCREENS AND CONTROLS

Stock recon centre: The starting point for creating a preview and reopening
saved runs. Head office: Control > Stock > Stock recon centre. Branch workspace:
Stock > Counting > Balancing.

Run: The saved result of one preview, with its site, dates, area, options and
lines. A saved run is a snapshot, not a live view of every later PumpIT change.
Give it a useful name, for example "Shop - week starting 19 October".

Preview the balancing: Reads the source and saves the calculated run in Agora.
It does not change PumpIT stock. Creating a preview is therefore a saved action,
even though no stock amendment is posted.

Proposals: Shows the calculated changes and outcomes. The main figures include
Shifts to amend, Chains to balance, Chains reported, Unrecorded issue, Shifts
over, Shifts short, Dormant shifts, Openings repaired and Hand balancing breaches.
Several figures link to their supporting rows. "Units moved" is the magnitude
of adjustments, not a saving or a quantity of recovered stock.

Outcome: The description of a row's proposed result, such as Balanced, Short,
No change, Blocked, Possible duplicate, Duplicate issue removed, Missing issue
captured or Hand amendment reversed. Even past-tense wording on a preview is
not proof of posting; check the commit state and result separately.

Chain detail: Opens the shifts behind a chain, with the source quantities,
proposed quantities, variance, breaks, flags and available crew/count evidence.
Matching letters and colours identify shifts that balance against each other.

Exceptions: The findings requiring attention. Within it, Exceptions, Review
and All rows separate substantive findings from routine count-drift review.
Type filters narrow the list; select a code badge for its explanation and action.
An exception is not always a block on committing.

Duplicate issues panel: Compares suspected repeated issues with their supporting
shifts. A clear duplicate and a possible duplicate have different consequences.
A release records an authorised decision with a reason; it does not delete an
issue or itself commit a stock change.

Global comparison: Compares chains within this run's site, period and areas.
It offers As counted and After proposed balancing, related-item candidates and
production context. Ratios are not applied in the current comparison. A raw
offset or matching quantity is a lead to investigate, not a confirmed correction.
For cross-area comparisons, the run must include those areas.

Every-site preview: Builds separate site runs for selected sites using one
week and every counting area. It is a way to organise previews; review and
commit each site's run individually. Global comparison is a different tool.

Options: Set correction limits, minimum active shifts, the permitted small
chain break and incomplete-day handling. A share of 0.50 means a correction
cap of half the stock on hand. Keep the agreed settings; a blocked result
needs explanation before anyone loosens a limit.

Amend / commit: Applies the selected eligible changes. In live mode it updates
PumpIT and saves the prior values in Agora's amendment history. Supported issue
changes include removing a clear duplicate and capturing a remaining over as
a missing issue. The original columns remain unchanged. In journal mode only
Agora's amendment history is written. Check the run's mode.

A commit that writes any rows marks the whole run committed, even when other
rows were unticked or skipped. Create a fresh preview for further amendments.
If every selected row is skipped, the run remains uncommitted.

Discard: Removes a preview, not a posted stock amendment. A committed run
cannot be discarded as if it had never happened.

Reverse: Undoes the changes recorded by that run using their saved prior
values. It requires a reason and the appropriate access. It refuses to overwrite
affected values changed since the run wrote them. It is not a blanket reset.

Reset to original: A separate write action that restores the run's covered
opening, closing, issue and POS values to the original basis, plus the previous
closing needed to anchor the window. It can remove manual amendments. It
records priors and is followed by a fresh preview. Use it only when restoring
that baseline is the intended action, not as a routine refresh button.
It leaves possible-duplicate holds and shifts protected by another run's
standing balancing amendment alone, reporting what was skipped and why.

Original vs amended variance: A separate comparison of the original and current
PumpIT figures over a freely selected period. It is useful after committing.
It includes that report's treatment of good waste against a short, so its
amended totals need not be identical to the balancing preview's raw variance.

PART 4: READING THE COMMON CODES

S10a / S10b: Opening below / above the preceding closing. Review the proposed
opening repair and the break in the chain.

D1: Clear duplicate issue. The proposal removes the duplicated issue, with
the related count changes. Check the supporting pair of shifts.

D2: Possible duplicate issue. The whole chain is held for review. Check the
issue evidence. An authorised release needs a reason and a new preview; the
release only stands while the issue remains the one reviewed. Changing only
an amended PumpIT issue does not erase the original evidence.

S1, U1, U2, U3, U4: Whole-pack over or signs of missing issues: sales exceed
available stock, closing exceeds available stock, sales without issues, or a
net over. Review the cause and any proposed missing-issue correction. These
findings do not all block balancing.

E1: Too little linked POS to explain the stock movement. Check the POS link
and posting before treating the difference as an ordinary stock shortage.

F1, F2, F3: A data/correction-limit fault; an issue with no corresponding sales
or count movement; or a conflict with existing amendments. These can hold back
the chain; check its actual held-back status and reason. Certain F1/F2 findings
do not block a nil-total chain. Resolve the stated cause before re-previewing.

S3 / S5: A whole-pack short / an issue outside expected pack multiples.
Check pack sizes, units and issue records.

N1 / N2 / L1: A pattern suggesting no physical count / mostly dormant stock /
persistent shorts. Review the underlying shifts and available evidence.

C1: Original/current count drift without a more specific count class. It is
informational and does not itself change commit eligibility or assign a loss.

Hand-balancing checks: S13 is an unresolved opening/closing mismatch; S12 flags
repair through the previous closing; S7 an over turned into a short; S8 an
increased short; S11 a changed latest closing; and S4, S4a, S4b a mis-keyed,
added or reduced issue. These describe existing amendments, separately from
what Agora proposes now.

PART 5: WORKED EXAMPLES

Example A - a counting difference moves between two shifts.

Shift A: Opening 100, Issued 20, POS 30, Closing 80.
Variance = 30 - (100 + 20 - 80) = -10: short by 10.

Shift B: Opening 80, Issued 0, POS 20, Closing 70.
Variance = 20 - (80 + 0 - 70) = +10: over by 10.

The combined variance is zero. Proposing A's closing as 90 and B's opening
as 90 makes each shift's variance zero. The first opening stays 100, the
last closing stays 70, and no issue is added. This illustrates count balancing.
The eligibility checks still decide whether a real chain can be amended.

Example B - a shortage remains.

If a continuous segment totals -5 on one shift and +3 on another, its total
is -2. Moving counts between them cannot remove that remaining 2-unit short.

Example C - a missing issue changes the total.

Opening 100, Issued 20, POS 30, Closing 95 gives a variance of +5.
Increasing the recorded issue from 20 to 25 makes the variance zero. That is
a 5-unit issue correction, not merely moving a closing count. In the current
live configuration Agora can propose this as "Missing issue captured"; review
its evidence and include the affected eligible rows in the commit.

PART 6: TUTORIAL - COMPLETE ONE AREA'S RECONCILIATION

1. Open Stock recon centre and select the site and counting area. Start with
   one area so the supporting shifts are manageable to review.

2. Choose From and read the calculated To date. Confirm that this is the
   period intended. Name the run. Leave Options at the agreed values and keep
   incomplete-day handling enabled for normal work.

3. Select Preview the balancing. Check the site, period, area and any days
   held back. A preview saves evidence in Agora; it has not amended PumpIT.

4. Read the summary. Identify the chains with proposed changes, the chains
   reported or held, and any unrecorded issues. Zero rows to amend can mean
   PumpIT already holds the proposed answer; it is not automatically an error.

5. Review Exceptions and the duplicate panel. Open the code explanation and
   supporting shifts. Resolve missing source information and blocks. When a
   possible duplicate is released, record the evidence and reason, then create
   a new preview. A release alone does not post anything.

6. Return to Proposals and open an item chain. Compare the original and proposed
   figures. Use Exceptions' Live open and Live close columns to review the
   current counts saved when this run was previewed. Check the anchored counts,
   the shifts being balanced,
   the remaining short and any issue correction. Confirm that the explanation
   fits the count sheets, issue records and POS evidence.

7. Use Global comparison if related items or areas may explain the pattern.
   Inspect candidates and production records as supporting evidence. Do not
   add several candidate matches together as separate recoveries or assume a
   raw offset proves a production conversion.

8. Select the intended changes. Eligible shifts start ticked. Ticks on other
   table pages still count; outcome and column filters can exclude rows from
   submission. Check the selected count and filter notice. The chain-row
   Amend action submits only that chain, regardless of other chains' ticks.

9. Read the confirmation and commit the reviewed selection if your role is
   authorised. Related shifts must be written together where required. Agora
   re-checks the source and can skip a chain if values moved or its required
   changes are not all included.

10. Read the actual commit result. Distinguish written rows from skipped
    rows and read every skip reason. A proposed "Balanced" result does not
    prove that the corresponding changes were successfully written. If any
    rows were written, use a fresh preview to work on remaining amendments;
    the original run is now committed, including after a one-chain Amend.

11. Open Original vs amended variance for the same site, area and dates.
    Compare the saved PumpIT result with the intended changes and investigate
    remaining differences. Use the run's amendment evidence for the exact
    writes; the comparison report has its own waste treatment.

12. Retain the run as the record of the work. If the commit was wrong, review
    Reverse and its reason requirement. If the intended job is to remove
    existing hand amendments, review Reset to original as a separate action.
    If data changed or a reversal is refused, investigate before trying again.

A run is ready to hand over when its scope is clear, its proposed changes have
been reviewed, its actual writes and skips are understood, and remaining
exceptions have an evidence-based explanation or an identified owner.

PART 7: RELATED WORK THAT IS SEPARATE FROM THIS TUTORIAL

Weekly investigation is a separate evidence-and-case workflow. It builds a
snapshot for a completed Monday-to-Sunday week, can use a wider control period,
and keeps investigation findings against that snapshot revision. Building it
again creates another revision. It is not the same as choosing any From date
for a balancing run, and its review/approval process is a separate procedure.

Counting-area maintenance, item/pack reference data and water-meter analysis
also have their own screens and access. Water meters use cumulative readings
and should not be interpreted as an ordinary shelf-stock count.

Virtual Items areas, such as lotto, airtime and electricity, are excluded from
physical stock balancing because they do not have the same issue/count basis.
